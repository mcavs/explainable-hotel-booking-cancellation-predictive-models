# [HBC Papers] Ampountolas (2025) Predicting hotel booking cancellations: a comprehensive machine learning approach  
  
* The dynamic nature of cancellation behavior during reservations leads to complex overbooking challenges that raise revenue expectations and risks.   
  
* While overbooking strategies can mitigate losses, they require accurate forecasting of cancellation rates to prevent customer dissatisfaction and reputational damage.   
  
* Despite the importance of accurate cancellation forecasts, literature on this topic remains limited, particularly concerning individual customer behavior and short-term cancellations close to the time of service (Antonio et al., 2017; Antonio et al., 2019).   
  
* Existing studies predominantly concentrate on the airline industry or often focus on aggregate cancellation ratios rather than predicting the likelihood of specific customers canceling their reservations.   
  
* This gap is especially critical for hotels, where last-minute cancellations leave little time for operational adjustments, resulting in unsolved inventory or the need for substantial discounts to fill rooms (Koide and Ishii, 2005; Chen et al., 2023).  
  
* Moreover, while designed to discourage last-minute changes, cancellation policies can have unintended consequences, such as damaging a hotel’s reputation or alienating potential customers (Antonio et al., 2017b).  
  
* Finally, the growing dominance of online booking platforms has further aggravated this issue, making it easier for customers to cancel reservations or engage in multiple booking practices. While beneficial for customers, this increased flexibility imposes significant burdens on hoteliers, who must adapt quickly to minimize losses and avoid unsold inventory.   
  
* This study addresses these challenges by demonstrating how advanced ML models, including hybrid approaches, can effectively predict cancellations at the individual customer level.   
  
* By leveraging real-world PMS data and a combination of advanced algorithms, this research not only enhances forecasting accuracy but also provides actionable insights for managing high-stakes scenarios, such as peak demand periods or last-minute cancellations.   
  
* Simple statistical models can be insufficient in a dynamic environment characterized by rapidly changing customer behavior and external factors such as economic shifts, global events, and public health crises.   
  
* By understanding factors such as seasonality, booking timing, customer demographics, and even macroeconomic indicators, hoteliers can adapt to evolving market trends and customer behaviors with greater agility.   
  
* The proposed approach enhances the accuracy of cancellation predictions and contributes to more effective revenue management strategies. For example, hotels can implement targeted interventions, such as personalized communication or flexible policies, to reduce cancellations and minimize revenue loss.
