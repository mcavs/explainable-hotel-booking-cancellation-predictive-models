# [HBC Papers] Mir et al. (2026) Decoding booking cancellations: quantitative insgihts amd theoretical advances in tourism behavior  
  
* Differently from other economic activities, the temporal distance between purchasing (booking) and consumption (stays) of the tourism services, also requires the analysis and understanding of purchasing behavior.   
  
* This is crucial for the design of marketing campaigns, revenue management, and other operational strategies.   
  
* More precisely, booking dates are the key moment for many tourism services and companies, such as transportation and accommodation (Chan et al., 2017; Wen et al., 2020).   
  
* Understanding and modeling bookings, instead of stays, implies a relevant improvement regarding the match between the variables under study and the time of consumption decision (Boto-Garcia et al., 2021).   
  
* Considering booking behavior, the increasing use of online distribution channels among tourists, and the changes in their behavior towards more independent planning (Deya-Tortella et al., 2022), has promoted the proliferation of flexible booking cancellation policies.   
  
* The challenging recent years—marked by pandemics, international conflicts, and other global disruption—led to a rise in uncertainty among prospective tourists. This, in turn, translated into a significant increase in the share of hotel bookings made under flexible cancellation policies (Kim et al., 2023). This option has become common for many booking channels, indicating a potential customer’s behavioral chance looking for more flexible and less risky decisions (Masiero et al., 2020).  
  
* The extension of cancellation policies might have also led to the emergence of strategic adaptive booking behavior among some consumers. That is, consumers who book while taking advantage of the possibility to cancel, and who monitor price fluctations with the intention to cancel and rebook when price dynamics allow for better deals (Chen et al., 2011).   
  
* Most research has focused on forecasting cancellations, typically adopting ML techniques (Chen et al., 2023; Yoo et al., 2024). However, these studies often fall short in providing a descriptive understanding of the determinants of cancellation behavior.   
  
* One of the main problems faced by researchers is the difficulty in accessing real data on cancellations. In fact, most of the extant literature focuses on data for the same hotel or chain, or through the same booking channel (Ampountolas, 2025; Sanchez-Medina et al., 2020).   
  
* Another stream of research, aiming to overcome this scarcity of data, has opted for experimental designs to explore the determinants and potential effects of cancellation policies on consumer behavior. Among them, Masiero et al. (2020) adopt a discrete choice experiment to analyze consumer preferences for different cancellation rate options, and the moderating role of risk attitudes of buyers.   
  
* Moreover, Kim et al. (2023) develops two experiments to investigate the factors that influence cancellation policy choices by customers, paying special attention to psychological mechanisms, situational cues trigger, and risk tolerance.   
  
* Chen et al. (2011) explores the impact of cancellation fees and deadlines on different categories of hotel customers.   
  
* While these studies provide valuable perspectives, there remains a lack of integrative theoretical work that explains not only why travelers cancel but also when they choose to do. Regarding the why, existing research tends to isolate psychological mechanisms from economic structures, offering fragmented explanations of consumer decision-making; and as for the when, the literature overlooks how consumers strategically adapt to changing market conditions.   
  
* The methodological contribution of our paper lies in the estimation of a time-to-event (hazard) model. This approach allows us to analyze bookings’ duration (i.e., how long a reservation remains active before being canceled or completed) and the likelihood of cancellation over time.   
  
* While hazard models have been employed in other sectors characterized by cancellation risks—such as freight shipments and airline ticket bookings (Iliescu et al., 2008; Zhao et al., 2020)—to the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first application of a such framework to the accommodation industry.
