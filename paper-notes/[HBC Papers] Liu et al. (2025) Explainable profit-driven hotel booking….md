# [HBC Paper] Liu et al. (2025) Explainable profit-driven hotel booking cancellation prediction based on heterogeneous stacking-based ensemble classification  
  
* Since hotel room reservations are highly perishable, and cancellations introduce uncertainty over if, when, and at which rates rooms are booked again, such cancellations negatively impact the profit of hotels (Chu, 2009; Heo and Lee, 2009; Saito et al., 2019).   
  
* Previous studies have indicated that if the hotel does not adopt proactive retention or win-back strategies, it will lead to a 20% decline in hotel revenue (Sierag et al., 2015).   
  
* Therefore, accurate hotel booking cancellation prediction, deployed to identify potential cancellations early on, can be an effective tool to optimize hotel revenue management (Webb, 2016).   
  
* In addition, the factors that cause consumers to cancel hotel bookings have become more complex. Beyond predicting cancalletions accurately, it is also essential for hotels to analyze why customers cancel hotel reservations to help hotels formulate targeted measures.   
  
* By leveraging the HBCPs, hotels can proactively engage with guests deemed at high risk of cancellation, forge a personal connection by showing interest and offering customized incentives such as tailored trip suggestions, exclusive deals, or flexible booking conditions to mitigate cancellation probabilities.   
  
* Such predictions into Revenue Management Systems significantly augments the precision of inventory and pricing strategies, enabling revenue optimization through more accurate demand forecasting.   
  
* Revenue managers can exploit these predictive insights for strategic demand management, including determining optimal overselling levels or deciding on the acceptance of late check-ins based on projected cancellations.  
  
* Furthermore, a dynamic adjustment of cancellation policies, informed by predictiors of cancellation, allows for policies that are responsive to variables such as booking lead times and customer geographical origin.   
  
* This adaptive strategy enables the nuanced application of cancellation penalties, calibrated to the likelihood of cancellation, thereby minimizing risk while avoiding the alienation of non-deal-seeking guests.   
  
* HBCP typically constitutes a binary classification problem, which can be solved through supervised learning methods.  
  
* Satu et al. (2020) investigated the HBCP performance of multiple classifiers.   
  
* Saputro and Nanang (2021) used RF and extremely randomized trees to predict hotel booking cancellarions and successfully identified 79% of cancelled hotel bookings.  
  
* Chen et al. (2022) used CatBoost to predict hotel booking cancellations and found that its performance better than LR and KNN.   
  
* Andriawan et al. (2020) conducted HBCP based on RF and attained better performance than ERT, CatBoost and LightGBM.  
  
* While decision-makers generally expect higher returns from more accurate HBCP models, the costs and benefits associated with correct or incorrect identification of a canceller or non-canceller are asymmetric.   
  
* Accurately identifying the cancellers will help the hotel take proactive retention measures to discourage customers from cancelling bookings and thus avoid facing lost revenues and incremental costs to fill up the accommodation.   
  
* Accurate prediction of non-cancellers can avoid the losses caused by unnecessary retention measures taken by the hotel.   
  
* Therefore, evaluating HBCP classifiers should rely on performance measures that measure profit instead of mere accuracy to align model evaluation with business goals and manager expectations.   
  
* To the best of our knowledge, only one previous study has focused on profit-driven HBCP (Liu et al., 2023). Our research presents a novel approach for profit-driven HBCP through an interpretable profit-optimized heterogeneous stacking-based ensemble classifier.
