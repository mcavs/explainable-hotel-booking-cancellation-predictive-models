# [HBC Papers] Yoo et al. (2023) Predicting hotel booking cancellation with machine learning techniques  
  
* Accurate demand forecasting has become an essential aspect of revenue management because the industry is highly affected by external factors such as natural disasters, economic volatility, political instabilities, and constantly faces uncertainty.   
  
* More scholars in the hospitality and tourism field are showing increased interest and attempting to apply advanced data analytical techniques in their research (Antonio et al., 2019; Kwon et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2021; Sanchez-Medina et al., 2020).  
  
* In this study, we explore how machine learning techniques can be helpful in making forecasts for hotel room demand.   
  
* This is especially difficult because the demand for hotel rooms is not simply a function of the number of reservations but also involves estimates for the rate of cancellations, which can vary substantially based on exıgenous factors beyond the hotel’s control—time of year, political instability, foreign exchange rates, natural disasters—as well as company policy.   
  
* In fact, it has been found that hotels lose one-fifth of their revenue as a result of room cancellations (Sierag et al., 2015).   
  
* Since cancellations play a significant role in determining net revenue, it is paramount that hotels understand cancellation behaviors and how their policy choices can enhance their revenue management approach.   
  
* Even among existing hospitality studies, booking cancellations are mainly considered as a regression problem, and traditional statistical methods are used (Sanchez-Medina et al., 2020).   
  
* To reduce the research gap in predicting hotel booking cancellations using ML techniques, this study applied hotel data from the property management system in the format of detailed booking data instead of time-series aggregated data.   
  
* Additionally, this study attempts to include more variables for modeling to increase the prediction power and to identify cancellation drivers from the classification prediction models.   
  
* By exploring a wide range of variables from a hotel property data management system, this study aims to determine the main cancellation drivers from the classification prediction models.   
  
* Forecasting demand accurately is challenging for hospitality operations as the environment is complex and highly sensitive to fluctuations (Li et al., 2018).   
  
* Hotel booking cancellations can impact customer service, so it is important to handle cancellation situations promptly and efficiently to maintain good customer relationships and reduce customer complaints (Sanchez-Medina et al., 2020; Talluri and van Ryzin, 2004).   
  
* Consequently, understanding and forecasting cancellations is important for hotels to optimize their revenue management strategies, ensure room availability, and reduce the potential for overbooking.   
  
* By accurately forecasting cancellation rates, hotel can adjust their room rates, occupancy levels, and marketing strategies to maximize their revenue and minimize any potential losses.   
  
* Hotel booking cancellation has progressed significantly due to changes in customer preferences, technology and industry practices.   
  
* In the past, cancellations were often difficult and more complicated, with hotels charging steep fees or not allowing cancellation policies to better accommodate guests (Riasi et al., 2019).  
  
* One of the key factors driving this change is the rise of online booking: a significant ratio of cancellations is from deal-seeking customers who search for the best available price and deals (Chen and Xie, 2013).   
  
* Another important factor has been the growth of the sharing economy platforms, such as Airbnb. These platforms typically offer more flexible cancellation policies, which puts pressure on hotels to remain competitive by improving their policies (Riasi et al., 2019).   
  
* As a result, hotels have become more competitive in terms of pricing and policies, including offering more flexible cancellation policies (Chen et al., 2011). As a part of a proactive cancellation policy, overbooking is a practice in the hotel industry to reduce the risk generated by unexpected no-shows (Talluri and van Ryzin, 2004).   
  
* Other hotel policies have increased revenue by charging penalties or encouraging customers to cancel earlier so hotels have enough time to sell their inventory to other guests (Chen et al., 2011). While most hotels have cancellation policies in place, they lack consistency across different hotel rankings and segments (Chen and Xie, 2013).   
  
* Hotels may choose to implement a no-cancellation policy where guests are not allowed to cancel their reservations at all. While this policy may benefit the hotels by reducing the risk of empty rooms and lost revenue, it also includes a few drawbacks.   
  
* A no-cancellation policy can be frustrating for guests who may have unexpected changes in their plans or unforeseen circumstances, which may influence customer satisfaction, lead to negative reviews, and harm the hotel’s reputation.   
  
* Further, it can also lead to lost revenue from potential guests who may choose not to book at the hotel due to the inflexibility of the policy. A no-cancellation policy can also limit the hotel’s flexibility to adjust to changing circumstances.   
  
* For example, if there is a sudden drop in demand, the hotel may not be able to adjust its room rates or promotions to attract more bookings (Chen and Xie, 2013).   
  
* Generally, the more rigid the hotel cancellation policy is, the more negative impact it may have on the overall performance of a hotel. Overall, hotel room cancellation policies are known to considerably affect a customer’s purchase decision, so the importance of these policies in the hotel industry is significant (Smith et al., 2015).   
  
* Predictive modeling is an emerging and innovative research method in the context of hospitality and tourism that makes predictions about the future based on historical data (Lee et al., 2021).  
  
* Some of the more traditional methods used in predicting hotel demand include time series models, econometric regression models, and linear-programming-based models, which are performed using historical data, advanced booking data, or both combined (Weatherford and Kimes, 2003; Weatherford et al., 2001; Pereira, 2016).  
  
* Recent innovations in technology and forecasting algorithms using different methods with detailed hotel booking data have been gaining popularity because they are more accurate and can develop classification prediction models (Antonio et al., 2019a, b; Falk and Vieru, 2018; Morales and Wang, 2010).   
  
* Morales and Wang (2010) investigated the problem of forecasting cancellation rates and determined that the predictors that drive cancellations change during different stages of the booking horizon. Their study treated cancellation rates as a regression problem, and results identified that estimation tree and kernel methods are favorable for forecasting cancellation rates.   
  
* More recent studies used classification algorithms and treated cancellation rates as a classification problem. Falk and Vieru (2018) examined the factors that affect cancellation behavior in hotel bookings. The study used variables such as length of stay, hotel category, booking lead time, arrival month, and country of residence, using a probit model with cluster-adjusted standard errors. Study results showed that online booking cancellation rates are higher than those of offline bookings and travel agency bookings.   
  
* Antonio et al. (2017, 2019a) used uncensored data from four resort hotels to test booking cancellation prediction using the ML method. Antonio et al. (2019b) also used ML models, and all three studies used more than 30 variables and demonstrated building booking cancellation prediction models with high-accuracy results.   
  
* Sanchez-Medina et al. (2020) used ML with 13 independent variables and concluded consistent suggestions from Antonio et al. (2017, 2019a, 2019b) research that personal name records data is useful for forecasting individual hotel cancellations.   
  
* Since we found that country of residence, ADR, and the lead time play an important role in the accurate prediction of cancellation rates, hotels can use this information in their cancellation policies to encourage their guests to maintain their reservations.   
  
* For example, hotels can offer some additional services depending on the ADR guests pay or offer different penalty fees depending on the lead time of their reservation.   
  
* Hotels may also consider designing their cancellation policies by implementing deeper customization and segmentation.   
  
* Hotels may consider dynamically charging different cancellation fees according to the country of residence rather than charging a fixed fee for every guest, since it turned out to be a significant factor in cancellation behavior.
