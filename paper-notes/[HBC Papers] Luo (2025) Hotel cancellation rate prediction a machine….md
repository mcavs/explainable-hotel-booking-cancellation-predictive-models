# [HBC Papers] Luo (2025) Hotel cancellation rate prediction: a machine learning based prediction model  
  
* However, it is precisely because of the convenience of booking that an increasing number of people are canceling their hotel bookings, which has brought a series of problems to hotels (Park et al., 2007).   
  
* First, frequent cancellations increase the hotel’s labor costs. Second, the hotel’s inventory is less than the actual situation, resulting in profit losses for the hotel.   
  
* In addition, the actual number of customers in the hotel does not match the estimate, resulting in a waste of hotel human resources. It can be seen that the phenomenon of a surge in hotel reservations not only affects the consumer experience, but also has significant negative impacts on hotel operations and profitability.   
  
* It is estimated that hotels lose about 20% of their revenue because the room reservation management system does not take into account the cancellation of reservations (Sierag et al., 2015). Therefore, hotels must understand short-term demand in advance and plan inventory reasonably to maximize occupancy rates.  
  
* However, literature available on cancellation forecasting within the industry is underdeveloped (Zakhary et al., 2011), and little is known about the reasons that lead guests to cancel or how to avoid it (Hajibaba et al., 2016).   
  
* Antonio et al. (2017) achieved a detection accuracy of over 90%, but there is still room for improvement, and no research has yet conducted a comparative analysis of the cancellation rates of urban hotels and resort hotels, so this paper aims to predict the hotel checkout rate of two city hotels and resort hotels using multiple ML methods and select the model with the highest prediction accuracy to achieve higher prediction accuracy (Sánchez-Medina et al., 2020).   
  
* This study evaluates the performance of 8 ML models in the hotel cancellation rate prediction task using ROC-AUC. The results show that the ensemble model XGBoost significantly outperforms the single model with 0.9808 AUC and 98.48% accuracy.  
  
* Our analysis shows that key features such as deposit type, booking lead time, and special needs are closely related to cancellation rates. For example, non-refundable deposits can effectively reduce the risk of default, while the longer the lead time, the higher the probability of cancellation.   
  
* Based on these findings, hotels can adopt strategies such as dynamic pricing, flexible resource allocation, and customer behavior intervention, such as setting an “early bird discount + high deposit” combination for long lead time orders, or improveness customer stickiness by meeting special needs, thereby optimizing operational efficiency and reducing revenue losses.
